### **Top 10 Email Account Security Recommendations** 📪

(From Your Trusted IT Partner)

1. **Use Strong, Unique Passwords**
    

- Never reuse your email password for any other site. Make it long, complex, and hard to guess.
    

2. **Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**
    

- Always turn on MFA (app, SMS, or hardware key). It’s the #1 way to stop stolen passwords from being enough to break in.
    

3. **Be Wary of Phishing Emails**
    

- Don’t click links or open attachments from unknown senders. When in doubt, verify by calling or messaging the sender directly.
    

4. **Update Recovery Options**
    

- Keep your backup email and phone number current to recover your account if it’s ever locked or compromised.
    

5. **Check Login Activity Regularly**
    

- Review sign-in history to spot suspicious logins. Most providers (Microsoft, Google) show recent devices and IPs.
    

6. **Use a Reputable Spam Filter**
    

- Let your IT provider or email service block suspicious or malicious emails before they reach your inbox.
    

7. **Encrypt Sensitive Emails**
    

- When sending confidential data, use built-in encryption tools (like Microsoft 365 Message Encryption) or a secure portal.
    

8. **Keep Devices Secure**
    

- Make sure laptops, phones, and tablets that access email have up-to-date OS patches, antivirus, and a lock screen.
    

9. **Sign Out of Shared or Public Devices**
    

- Always log out of webmail when using public or shared computers. Don’t save passwords in browsers on these machines.
    

10. **Educate and Train Regularly**
    

- Stay alert for new phishing tactics. Regular security awareness training is essential — even for tech-savvy users.
    

---

###  📬 **Bonus Pro Tips** 📬

**Microsoft 365 Users**

- Turn on **Advanced Threat Protection (ATP)** if available — it scans links & attachments in real time.
    

**Gmail Users**

- Use Google’s **Security Checkup** to review account health, third-party app access, and recovery methods.
    

**Company Email**

- Avoid forwarding work email to personal accounts — this bypasses corporate security tools.
